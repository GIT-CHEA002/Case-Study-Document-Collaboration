# What Does Agile Contribute to Mentcare in Real Life?

Agile contributes to Mentcare in several important ways. Its main value is not only faster software development, but also making the system more practical, safer, and more suitable for real healthcare environments.

---

## 1. Better Alignment with Real Clinical Workflows

The first contribution is making the software more aligned with how doctors and nurses actually work.

A developer might think that taking five steps to view a patient's medication is normal. However, during a real consultation, those five steps can significantly slow down the clinical workflow.

Clinician feedback can help the development team:

- Reduce unnecessary steps
- Prioritize important information
- Improve screen layout
- Simplify navigation
- Make critical information easier to find 
- Improve the overall clinical workflow

The key idea is:

> **Software should adapt to clinical work, not force clinicians to adapt to inefficient software.**

---

## 2. Early Problem Detection

The second contribution is **early problem detection**.

If a system is developed for a long period and only shown to the hospital at the end of the project, major workflow problems may be discovered too late.

At that point, fixing them can be expensive and difficult.

With Agile, an early prototype or increment can reveal problems within the first few weeks.

For example:

```text
Early Prototype
      ↓
Clinician Testing
      ↓
Workflow Problem Detected
      ↓
Feedback
      ↓
Early Correction
```

This reduces the risk of developing a large system based on incorrect assumptions.

---

## 3. Support for Patient Safety

The third contribution is supporting **patient safety**.

For example, a doctor may provide feedback that:

- An allergy warning is not clear enough
- A suicide-risk indicator is difficult to notice
- High-risk information is displayed too late
- Medication information is difficult to find

The development team can improve these features in the next iteration.

In this case, Agile does more than improve the user interface.

It may also reduce the risk of clinicians missing important information.

A simple relationship is:

```text
Clinical Feedback
      ↓
Better Information Presentation
      ↓
Lower Risk of Missing Critical Information
      ↓
Safer Clinical Decisions
```

---

## 4. Better Adaptation to Healthcare Changes

The fourth contribution is **adaptability**.

Healthcare environments can change over time.

Examples include changes in:

- Patient examination processes
- Hospital policies
- Clinical workflows
- Medical forms
- Reporting requirements
- Regulatory requirements
- Administrative procedures

A system developed incrementally is generally easier to adapt than a system designed rigidly from the beginning.

Agile supports continuous refinement when new needs appear.

---

## 5. Reduced Project Cost and Risk

The fifth contribution is reducing project costs and risks.

If a feature is unsuitable, Agile helps detect the problem early.

For example:

> Fixing a Patient Summary after Sprint 2 is much easier than redesigning the entire system after it has already been deployed to hundreds of clinics.

Early feedback reduces:

- Rework
- Development waste
- Deployment risk
- Cost of major redesign
- Risk of user rejection

The general principle is:

> **The earlier a problem is discovered, the easier and cheaper it is to fix.**

---

## 6. Stronger User Engagement

Finally, Agile encourages continuous user involvement.

Doctors and nurses do not only appear at the beginning of the project to provide requirements and then disappear.

Instead, they participate throughout the development process by:

- Reviewing prototypes
- Testing working increments
- Giving feedback
- Clarifying requirements
- Identifying workflow problems
- Suggesting improvements
- Validating whether the system supports real clinical needs

This is especially important in healthcare because:

> **Developers understand technology, while clinicians understand the realities of patient care.**

Agile helps connect these two areas of expertise.

---

# Real-Life Contribution Summary

Agile contributes to Mentcare by improving both the development process and the final healthcare system.

Its major contributions include:

1. **Better clinical workflow**
2. **Earlier problem detection**
3. **Improved patient safety**
4. **Better adaptability**
5. **Lower development risk and cost**
6. **Continuous clinician involvement**

The overall relationship can be summarized as:

```text
Clinician Feedback
       ↓
Requirement Refinement
       ↓
Better Software
       ↓
Better Workflow
       ↓
Safer and More Effective Patient Care
```

---

# Key Conclusion

The most important real-life contribution of Agile to Mentcare is that it keeps the software closely connected to actual clinical needs.

Instead of developers making all decisions based only on assumptions, doctors and nurses continuously evaluate the system and help refine it.

A useful summary is:

> **Agile helps transform real clinical experience into better healthcare software.**

Or more simply:

> **Agile helps Mentcare become more usable, adaptable, cost-effective, and supportive of safer patient care.**
